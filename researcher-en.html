Transport Research: Longreads

These studies were created as part of the "Flyover" project, while I worked as a data journalist at the Moscow Traffic Control Center (2022–2026).

I was responsible for producing longreads from start to finish: analyzing and visualizing data, writing text, and prototyping web design.

In addition, I developed an ethical code for the media. For example, each article includes a "How We Worked with the Data" section, ensuring methodological transparency and reproducibility of the research.

In total, I prepared 14 full-length studies within the project.

Feedback from Greek Experts on Maria Kazakova's Presentation at the "Spatial Changes and Social Opportunities in the 21st-Century City" Conference in Athens (Greece), 2026.

  • Lila Leontidou, Professor Emerita of Geography and European Culture at the Faculty of Humanities of the Greek Open University, noted:

      “Southern researchers shaped a pessimistic axis of the conference agenda, covering topics such as spatial and social inequalities, polarization, segregation, marginalization, migration, and overtourism. The optimistic axis of the conference, on the other hand, was shaped by presentations on smart cities and the digital society, urban communities, and social movements. To a large extent, the optimistic agenda was set by Northern researchers, who demonstrated how digital methods help ensure safety in cities. I would particularly like to highlight the presentation on traffic cameras in Russia, as it showed that smart cities can indeed serve the public good.”

During her presentation, Maria Kazakova compared traffic conditions and media coverage of traffic cameras in Athens and Moscow, which managed to engage even those attendees who had never been to Russia.

  • Manos Spyridakis, Social Anthropologist, Head of the Department of Sociology at the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens, and Chair of the Commission on Urban Anthropology of the International Union of Anthropological and Ethnological Sciences (IUAES) — endorsed Maria Kazakova's approach:

      “At this conference, we aimed to reflect how social changes manifest themselves through space. That is why we were especially grateful for the comparative analysis. It was very important for us and allowed us to learn a great deal. In my view, the comparison between Greece and Russia was the key case study of this conference.”
